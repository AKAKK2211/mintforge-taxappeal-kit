How to appeal your property taxes in Michigan

Appeal services keep ~30% of your first-year savings. You can do it yourself with evidence — most homeowners who protest with comparable sales win a reduction. This guide is a general framework; always verify deadlines and forms with your county assessor (official source below).

1. Know your number

Get your assessment notice and your county's effective tax rate. Compare your assessed value to what similar homes near you actually sold for in the last 6-12 months. If your assessment is 5-10%+ above the indicated market value, you have a case. Use the free calculator to see the potential annual savings.

2. Gather comparable sales

Pull 3-5 sold homes (not listings) from the same neighborhood, similar size and condition. Adjust for differences conservatively. This is the evidence that wins appeals — not opinions.

3. File before the deadline

Deadline for Michigan: usually by the Tuesday after the first Monday in March, or 35 days after the notice (varies). Missing it means waiting a year — mark it now. File with the March Board of Review / Michigan Tax Tribunal (on appeal) using your county's official form.

4. Make your case

Start with the informal review (most counties offer one) — bring your comps, be polite, show the math. If needed, take it to the formal hearing with the same evidence.

Michigan uses the March Board of Review; the Tax Tribunal is the next step.
